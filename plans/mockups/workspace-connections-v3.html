Workspace connections, on the idiom the rest of the app already uses

/templates/new and the new-workspace page say the same thing twice: a section is a bordered card with a heading pair, and everything choosable inside it is a tile — border, --r-item, a strong name over muted lines, a facts strip under a rule, and a selected state drawn as a 1 px inset ring. The connections panel was the one surface that instead used bare list rows with a trailing pill button each. These are the same connections, rebuilt as tiles.

  • Where each part comes from. The panel is .blueprint-selection — border, --r-card, 16 px pad, 14 px gap — with the same heading pair. The tile is .template-tile. The strip is .blueprint-machine-facts. The check is .blueprint-machine-check. Nothing here is a new invention.
  • The tile is the control. Like .template-tile, the whole card is one button. That is what removes the trailing pill from every row, which was the wall of five buttons.
  • One state, drawn as a ring. Connected is a green 1 px inset ring plus a check — the treatment the template tile already uses for selected. Focus keeps the accent ring, so blitz connections open still wins the eye.
  • The strip says the verb. Connected, Connect, Sign in, Needs a key — one word pair on the left, the fine print on the right, at the machine card's 10 px.
  • No custody words. injected and proxied never appear. That already shipped.
  • A or B. A is the stacked tile and holds long provider names and a date. B is the template grid, denser, but "Google Workspace" has to shorten. A is the safer default at 339 px; B earns its place when the pane is wide.
